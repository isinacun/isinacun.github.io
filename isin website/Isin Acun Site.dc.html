Job Market Paper

Who Bears the Burden of Digital Service Taxes?

with Johannes Kasinger & Nadia Abou Nabout

Digital service taxes were designed to make Big Tech pay. Using an advertiser-level Google Ads panel — 16,532 advertisers across 29 European countries — and Google's staggered DST invoice rollout, we show the tax is passed through near-completely to local advertisers: ad prices rise 3.4%, almost exactly the 3.2% statutory rate, while quantities stay flat. The first causal estimate of DST incidence in digital advertising.

with Kasinger & Abou Nabout

DSTs meant to tax Big Tech are passed through near-completely to local advertisers: +3.4% ≈ the 3.2% statutory rate, while clicks and budgets stay flat.

Trade · Marketplaces

Not so De Minimis: Tariffs & Cross-Border Trade on Online Marketplaces

with Abou Nabout, Kaiser & Liaukonytė

Ending the $800 duty-free rule shrinks the market ≈6% — mostly lost cross-border sales, not redistribution. Foreign sellers lose 13–16% of weekly units.

UGC · Branding

The Dupe-licity Effect: Does Viral UGC Help or Hurt Premium Brands?

with Grzadziel, Brand, Ruckelshausen & Abou Nabout

Viral "dupe" videos don't cannibalize premium revenue — they extend it: ≈3 extra weeks of sales per product-country each year.
