Job Market Paper

Who Bears the Burden of Digital Service Taxes?

with Johannes Kasinger & Nadia Abou Nabout

Digital service taxes were designed to make Big Tech pay. Using an advertiser-level Google Ads panel — 16,532 advertisers across 29 European countries — and Google's staggered DST invoice rollout, we show the tax is passed through near-completely to local advertisers: ad prices rise 3.4%, almost exactly the 3.2% statutory rate, while quantities stay flat. The first causal estimate of DST incidence in digital advertising.

Read paper → Slides SSRN

About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Marketing at WU Vienna, where I study the economics of digital platforms — how taxes, tariffs, and viral content reshape who sells, who buys, and who bears the cost online. My work pairs large advertiser- and marketplace-level panels with causal designs (staggered DiD, event studies, PPML) to deliver the first credible estimates of policy effects in digital markets.

Before Vienna I completed an M.Sc. in Economics at the University of Mannheim and a B.Sc. at Middle East Technical University. I expect to graduate in 2027.
